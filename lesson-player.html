Bodies, Boundaries & Body Safety Ages 3–5

Learning About Personal Boundaries

A gentle, age right glimpse into how we help families talk about body safety, consent, and everyday choices in ways that feel calm instead of scary.

For parents & caregivers

This preview is written for you, the trusted adult. You can read it first, then come back to share pieces with your child in your own words, at your own pace.

With children ages 3 to 5, personal boundaries start with simple ideas: your body belongs to you, and you get to say yes or no to touch that is not needed for care or safety. Kids this age learn best through short, repeatable phrases and everyday moments, not long talks.

You might begin by naming the spaces on their body that are private, using real words in a calm, matter of fact tone. For example: “Your body is good, and there are parts that are just for you. We call those private parts.” Pair this with reminders that grown ups help with bodies only for care, health, or safety, and that they can always tell you if something ever feels confusing.

As you move through the day, you can gently practice asking and answering questions about touch: “Would you like a hug, or a high five?” If they say no, you honour it, so they see that no is a real answer. You can also help them practice speaking up by pretending a stuffed animal is unsure and needs their help to decide what feels okay.

When questions come up about other people’s bodies, you can respond without shame: “That is a great question. Everyone’s body is private and deserves respect. If you are ever unsure what is okay, you can always ask me.” This keeps you in the role of safe guide instead of a voice of panic or embarrassment.

Over time, these small, calm conversations build a steady message: your child’s body is good, they can come to you with anything, and you will listen. That steady safety, not fear, is what protects them as they grow.
